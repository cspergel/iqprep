Guides for parents

What each kind of IQ-style question looks like, how to explain it, and simple ways to practise at home.

  • Using IQ Prep well before a testA calm plan, what to avoid, and what to tell your child.
  • Words and ideasQuestions that ask a child to explain: how two things are alike, what a word means, what they know about the world, and what to do in everyday situations.
  • Shapes and spaceQuestions about seeing how shapes fit: copying a design with blocks, and choosing the pieces that make a shape without moving them.
  • Patterns and rulesQuestions about finding a rule from a few examples and using it: picture grids, balance scales, pictures that belong together, and math stories told out loud.
  • MemoryQuestions about holding a few things in mind for a moment and working with them: numbers forwards and backwards, pictures in order, letters and numbers sorted.
  • SpeedSimple tasks done quickly and accurately against a clock: copying symbols from a key, spotting a symbol in a row, finding animals on a busy page.
  • Naming and symbol learningExtra question types that are given less often: naming familiar things quickly, and learning a small picture language then reading it back.

Every puzzle in IQ Prep is original and generated on the spot; none are real test questions, and practice results are estimates, not IQ scores. The aim is familiarity, so a test measures your child’s thinking rather than their surprise. For questions about a particular assessment, ask the psychologist who gives it.
